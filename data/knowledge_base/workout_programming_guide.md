# AI Movement Coach Knowledge Base: Workout Programming Cheat Sheet

## 1. Muscle Hypertrophy

- **Volume:** 10–20+ sets per muscle group per week is effective, with some studies showing benefits for higher volumes (20+ sets)[1].
- **Intensity:** Moderate loads (60–85% 1RM). Work sets should be within 1–3 reps of muscular failure.
- **Frequency:** Each muscle group 2–3x/week. Frequency is less critical if total weekly volume is equated[2][3].
- **Programming:** 
  - 6–12 reps/set typical.
  - 3–5 sets/exercise.
  - Progression: Increase sets, reps, or load linearly as strength improves[4].
- **Exercises:** Compound lifts (squat, bench, deadlift, row, overhead press), plus isolation where needed.
- **Techniques:** Use progressive overload, incorporate occasional intensity techniques (drop sets, supersets) for advanced trainees.
- **Split:** Upper/lower, push/pull/legs, or full body.
- **Recovery:** 48h between sessions for a muscle group; combine with active recovery days.
- **References:** [1][2][3][4][5]

## 2. Flexibility & Range of Motion

- **Methods:** 
  - Mix **dynamic stretching** (before workouts) with **static stretching** (post-workout)[6][7].
  - **Active stretching, foam rolling**, and Yoga/Pilates[7].
  - Include mobility exercises (leg swings, arm circles, lunges, hip openers).
- **Frequency:** 5–7x/week for flexibility; at least 2–3x/week for meaningful gains.
- **Intensity:** Hold static stretches 15–60s, 2–4 sets per area.
- **Programming:** Add stretching at the end of workouts; dedicate one session per week entirely to mobility/flexibility.
- **Balance:** Target opposing muscle groups to ensure symmetrical flexibility[6].
- **Recovery:** Use foam rolling and gentle stretching on rest/active recovery days.
- **References:** [6][7][8]

## 3. Movement Quality & Complexity (Ido Portal Style)

- **Philosophy:** 
  - Train movement, not just muscles ("Isolation → Integration → Improvisation")[9].
  - Focus on developing body control, coordination, and adaptability.
- **Techniques:** Locomotion drills (bear crawl, crab walk), flow sequences, animal walks, hand balancing, hanging, advanced bodyweight patterns.
- **Programming:** 
  - Practice isolated skills first.
  - Integrate skills into complex flows.
  - Regularly improvise and play with movement.
- **Frequency:** 3–5x/week, often used as warm-up/cool-down or standalone session.
- **Intensity:** Moderate—emphasize quality, not quantity. Short "movement snacks" multiple times/day are encouraged.
- **Progression:** Increase complexity (degrees of freedom), not just sets/reps[9].
- **References:** [9][10][11]

## 4. Joint Health, Prehab & Rehab

- **Exercises:** 
  - Compound joint-friendly moves: Squat, step-up, row, shoulder press, leg extension, stability/balance drills (bosu ball, balance board)[12].
  - Include prehab movements popularized by KneesOverToesGuy (reverse sled drag, tibialis raises).
- **Programming:** 
  - 1–2x/week as dedicated joint health session or sprinkled after main workouts.
  - 30–60s per exercise, 2–3 rounds/week[12].
- **Prehab Focus:** Corrective exercise, slow eccentrics, isometrics, and controlled mobility work.
- **Recovery:** Add balance and agility work. Include water-based activity or gentle cardio for "off" days[13].
- **References:** [12][14][13]

## 5. Overall Vitality, Longevity & Balance

- **Principles:** 
  - Combine **strength, cardio, balance, flexibility, and mobility** training for healthy aging and practical movement[15][16].
  - Use full-body compound movements.
  - Prioritize activities improving cardiovascular health, balance (tai chi, single-leg drills), and bone density.
- **Frequency:** Most days of the week (4–6x) with varied intensity.
- **Intensity:** Moderate (RPE 5–7/10); avoid high impact unless already proficient.
- **Programming:** 
  - Tend towards moderate-high frequency, low-intermediate intensity[15][16].
- **Recovery:** Emphasize sleep, stress management, walking, and hydration.
- **References:** [15][16]

## 6. Mobility & Recovery Practices

- **Routine:** 
  - Include 1–3 mobility movements within every workout (active warm-up/cool-down)[17][18].
  - Add full mobility sessions 1–2x/week.
- **Techniques:** Flow-based drills, joint articulations, Controlled Articular Rotations (CARs), passive and active stretching.
- **Active Recovery:** Use walking, cycling, swimming, or yoga for off days[19].
- **Frequency:** Minimum 2–3x/week; daily is even better[17].
- **References:** [17][18][19]

## 7. Integration Strategy: Sample Balanced Weekly Plan

| Day          | Focus                                    |
|--------------|------------------------------------------|
| Mon          | Strength (Hypertrophy) + Mobility        |
| Tue          | Movement Quality/Complexity + Cardio     |
| Wed          | Joint Health/Prehab + Recovery Mobility  |
| Thu          | Strength (Hypertrophy) + Flexibility     |
| Fri          | Movement Complexity + Cardio/Balance     |
| Sat          | Full-Body Longevity/Mobility Session     |
| Sun          | Active Recovery (walk, stretch, foam roll)|

- **Key Points:** 
  - Rotate focus areas, but integrate mobility and movement quality daily.
  - Use "movement snacks"—short, frequent mobility bursts—especially for sedentary individuals.
  - Prioritize weakest areas early in the week.
- **References:** [18][17][15][2][9]

**For programming, always individualize:** Progression, regression, and rest should be customized based on user age, training status, past injury, and preferences.
